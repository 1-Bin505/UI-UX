Tihar & Chhath

A five-day festival of light, lamps, kinship and reverence—honouring crows, dogs, cows, Laxmi, the self and siblings.

A sun-and-river observance dedicated to Surya and Chhathi Maiya, marked by devotion, fasts and offerings at dusk and dawn.

Tihar · Day 1

Kaag Tihar

Kaag Tihar is the first day of the five-day Tihar festival celebrated in Nepal. On this day, people worship crows, which are believed to carry messages between the living and the world of the dead. Families offer grains, sweets, and other food to crows on rooftops, balconies, and doorsteps. Crows are considered messengers of Yama, the god of death. Feeding them is believed to keep sorrow and bad luck away from the family.

Tihar is also known as Yamapanchak, meaning "five days of Yama." The tradition of worshipping animals comes from old Hindu beliefs. Tihar is the second biggest festival in Nepal after Dashain. Kaag Tihar teaches us to respect birds, care for animals, and live peacefully with nature.

Tihar · Day 2

Kukur Tihar

Kukur Tihar is the second day of the five-day Tihar festival. On this day, every dog, whether a family pet or a stray dog, is treated with love and respect. Dogs receive a red tika on their forehead, a garland of marigolds around their neck, and delicious food. The Newar community calls this celebration Khicha Puja.

This day is celebrated to thank dogs for their loyalty, friendship, and watchfulness. In Hindu mythology, dogs are believed to be guardians connected with Yama, the god of death, and the gates of the afterlife. Today, pictures of decorated dogs are shared across the internet, making Kukur Tihar famous around the world.

Tihar · Day 3 (morning)

Gai Puja

Gai Tihar is celebrated on the third day of the five-day Tihar festival. On this day, people worship cows by washing them, putting tika on their foreheads, and decorating them with garlands. They are also given fresh grass and tasty treats. People clean their homes and decorate their doorways.

Cows are considered a form of Laxmi, the goddess of wealth, because they provide milk and help nourish people and farmland. Worshipping cows is believed to bring happiness and prosperity to the home. Gai Tihar is celebrated in the morning, followed by Laxmi Puja in the evening.

Tihar · Day 3 (evening)

Laxmi Puja

Laxmi Puja is celebrated on the evening of the third day of the Tihar festival. On this day, people clean and decorate their homes with oil lamps, candles, and colourful lights. They also make beautiful Rangoli patterns at their doorways. Families worship Goddess Laxmi with flowers, incense, and sweets.

Goddess Laxmi is believed to visit clean and brightly lit homes, bringing wealth, happiness, and good fortune. The lights also represent the victory of good over evil and light over darkness. According to Hindu mythology, Goddess Laxmi appeared during the churning of the cosmic ocean. Tihar is also known as the festival of lights, and Laxmi Puja is one of its most important celebrations.

Tihar · Day 4

Mha Puja

The fourth day of Tihar is celebrated in different ways. The Newar community celebrates Mha Puja by worshipping themselves. Families create beautiful mandalas using oil, coloured powder, and rice. They decorate them with lamps, flowers, and fruits and offer prayers for each family member. In other communities, people worship oxen, known as Goru Puja, or celebrate Govardhan Puja.

Mha Puja teaches people to respect themselves and wish for a healthy body and peaceful mind. This day also marks the beginning of Nepal Sambat, the Newar New Year. Govardhan Puja celebrates the story of Lord Krishna lifting a hill to protect people and cattle from heavy rain and floods. These traditions teach us the importance of self-respect, protection, and gratitude.

Tihar · Day 5

Bhai Tika

Bhai Tika is the fifth and final day of the Tihar festival. On this day, sisters worship their brothers and pray for their long and healthy lives. Sisters apply colourful tika on their brothers' foreheads, put garlands around their necks, and offer them fruits and sweets. Brothers and sisters also exchange gifts and blessings.

Bhai Tika celebrates the love and strong bond between brothers and sisters. The oil circle made around brothers is believed to protect them from harm and death. According to a popular legend, Goddess Yamuna worshipped her brother Yama, the god of death, with tika and food. In return, Yama blessed brothers who receive tika from their sisters with long lives. Bhai Tika falls on the second day of the bright fortnight of Kartik.

Tihar · Nights of Laxmi Puja and Bhai Tika

Deusi-Bhailo

Deusi Bhailo is a popular tradition celebrated during the Tihar festival. Groups of people visit different houses in the evening, singing songs and dancing with madal drums. Boys and men usually sing Deusi, while girls and women sing Bhailo. At the end of each visit, they give blessings to the family. In return, hosts offer money, fruits, rice bread, and other gifts.

Deusi Bhailo spreads happiness, love, and good wishes among people. The songs bring blessings of good health and prosperity to every home. The money collected is sometimes used for community activities and social work. According to folk tradition, Deusi Bhailo is connected with the story of King Bali and Lord Vishnu's Vamana avatar. It is one of the most enjoyable and exciting traditions of Tihar.

Chhath · Day 3

Sandhya Arghya

Chhath Puja is a festival dedicated to the Sun God, Surya, and Chhathi Maiya. On this day, devotees observe a fast and visit rivers or ponds carrying bamboo baskets filled with fruits and traditional sweets. They stand in the water and offer water or milk to the setting sun. People also sing traditional Chhath songs during the celebration.

Chhath Puja is celebrated to thank the Sun God for supporting life on Earth. People also worship Chhathi Maiya and pray for the good health, happiness, and long life of their children. The tradition of sun worship comes from ancient Hindu beliefs. Chhath Puja is widely celebrated in Mithila, including Janakpur in Nepal, Bihar, and eastern Uttar Pradesh. It falls on Kartik Shukla Shashthi, six days after Diwali.

Chhath · Day 4

Usha Arghya

Usha Arghya is the final morning of Chhath Puja. Before sunrise, families gather near rivers or ponds with bamboo baskets filled with thekua, fruits, and sugarcane. Devotees stand facing the rising sun and offer water to Surya. After the prayers, they share prasad and break their fast, which is known as Parana.

This ritual is performed to thank the Sun God for bringing light and life to the world. People also pray to Chhathi Maiya for good health, prosperity, and the wellbeing of their children. Chhath is one of the oldest sun-worshipping festivals. Its traditions are connected with stories of Sita, Draupadi, and Karna. Devotees also follow strict rules, such as eating pure vegetarian food without onion and garlic and keeping the kitchen clean.
